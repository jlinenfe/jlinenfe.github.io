Miami Waterkeeper GIS & Data Analysis

Project Overview

In partnership with Miami Waterkeeper, I conducted spatial and data analyses to help evaluate sanitary sewer overflows and wastewater infrastructure issues across Miami-Dade County. The project involved compiling and organizing large environmental datasets, developing GIS-based visualizations, and analyzing the spatial and temporal distribution of sewage spills to better understand where incidents were occurring and how patterns changed over time.

Using GIS, RStudio, and environmental records, I developed maps and analytical products that transformed complex datasets into clear visual information that could support research, communication, and organizational decision-making.

Background

Sanitary sewer overflows can release untreated or partially treated wastewater into streets, waterways, and surrounding environments, creating potential risks to water quality and public health. Understanding where these events occur, how frequently they occur, and whether persistent geographic patterns exist can help organizations identify areas of concern and communicate infrastructure challenges to stakeholders.

This project brought together historical sewer spill records and spatial information to examine wastewater-related incidents across Miami-Dade County and place individual events within a broader geographic and historical context.

Objective

The primary objective of this project was to use data analytics and GIS to organize, visualize, and evaluate historical sanitary sewer overflow information. Specific objectives included:

  • Compile and clean historical sanitary sewer overflow records from multiple datasets
  • Map the geographic distribution of reported sewage spills across Miami-Dade County
  • Evaluate changes in spill occurrence and distribution through time
  • Compare spill locations with relevant wastewater infrastructure and regulatory boundaries
  • Develop maps and visualizations that clearly communicate complex environmental data
  • Provide Miami Waterkeeper with analytical products that could support research, outreach, and future investigations

Methods & Tools

The analysis combined data cleaning, spatial analysis, GIS mapping, and visualization. Historical records were standardized and organized before being joined with geographic information to allow individual incidents to be mapped and compared across space and time.

Spatial products were developed using GIS, while additional data processing and analysis were performed in RStudio. The resulting workflow allowed large tabular datasets to be converted into interpretable maps and figures that highlighted geographic patterns and areas warranting further investigation.

Results Summary

Spatial Distribution of Sewer Overflows

Historical sewer overflow records were mapped across Miami-Dade County to visualize where incidents occurred and identify geographic concentrations of reported spills. Mapping the dataset provided a clearer view of spatial patterns that are difficult to recognize from tabular records alone.

Historical & Temporal Analysis

Organizing the historical dataset by year allowed changes in sewage spill activity to be evaluated through time. This provided additional context for determining whether observed incidents represented isolated events or were part of longer-term patterns within the wastewater system.

Regulatory & Infrastructure Context

Spatial datasets were also compared with relevant wastewater infrastructure and regulatory information to place reported incidents within the broader management landscape. This helped connect individual spill records with the systems and geographic areas in which they occurred.

Applied GIS & Data Communication

A major component of the project involved converting complex environmental and infrastructure datasets into accessible maps and visualizations. These products provided Miami Waterkeeper with tools for communicating spatial patterns, supporting additional analyses, and identifying questions for future investigation.

Project Significance

This project demonstrates how GIS, data analytics, and environmental datasets can be integrated to support nonprofit and organizational decision-making. By converting large historical datasets into spatially explicit information, the analysis provided a more intuitive way to explore wastewater infrastructure issues and communicate patterns to both technical and non-technical audiences.
